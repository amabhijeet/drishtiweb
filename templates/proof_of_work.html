Proof of Work

A reviewer can return to the moment a possible pothole appeared in a road survey, watch the approach and check the available location evidence.

What the demonstration showed

7 timestamped candidates

The demonstration export recorded seven possible potholes. Each timestamp identifies a moment to inspect in the survey footage.

7 annotated photographs

The team supplied these photographs separately to illustrate what a flagged road surface looks like.

7 draft reports

The staff workspace showed seven reports awaiting a decision. Publication requires a separate review and approval.

Layers of a road survey

  1. Field capture. A surveyor records the route on a phone. Short video segments can upload during the journey or after connectivity returns.
  2. Candidate detection. Visual screening flags possible potholes for inspection.
  3. Evidence retention. Staff can revisit the footage, timestamp and available GPS context for an observation.
  4. Departmental review. A reviewer checks the observation against the recording and records a decision.
  5. Public reporting. Approved reports appear on the public map.

What comes next

A controlled road pilot is proposed to measure detection quality, location accuracy, processing time and how easily staff can review candidates.
